## Speedcubes vs. the original

The original Rubik's Cube was built to be a puzzle, not a racing machine. It is stiff, it catches when you turn a layer slightly off-axis, and its corners resist cutting. A speedcube keeps the same puzzle but changes almost everything about how it feels in the hand: smooth plastics, a looser internal structure, and adjustable hardware so you can tune it to your fingers.

You do not need a speedcube to learn the [beginner method](../../tutorials/3x3-beginner/). But if you plan to practice regularly, a good one makes every turn easier and every algorithm more reliable, and it costs far less than most people expect.

## Features worth understanding

### Magnets

Small magnets inside the pieces help each layer click into alignment. They reduce the number of times a layer stops a few degrees short of square, which is one of the most common causes of a lock-up. Magnets are standard on almost every serious speedcube, and many cubes let you swap or adjust their strength. Stronger magnets feel stable and controlled; weaker ones feel freer and faster but forgive less sloppy turning.

### Maglev

Maglev cubes replace the metal springs under the center caps with pairs of repelling magnets. The positioning magnets in the pieces work as usual, but the tensioning has no spring noise or spring friction, and the result is a very light, floaty feel. A maglev cube tends to be quiet and fast, but it can feel loose if you are not yet in the habit of turning cleanly. It is a matter of taste, not a requirement.

### Tensions and the core

Most speedcubes have adjustable tension: a spring and screw under each center cap that set how tightly the pieces are held together. Tighter feels stable and controlled, and looser feels fast and free. A cube that is too loose will pop; one that is too tight will feel sluggish and wear you out.

Some cubes add a magnetic ball core: magnets in a sphere at the center of the puzzle that pull on the corners, for a more stable, controllable turning feel. Whether a cube has a traditional core or a ball core, the thing to look for is that it turns smoothly and without grinding.

Try small adjustments, a quarter turn of a screw at a time, until the cube turns the way you like. Keep all six tensions about the same, or the cube will feel uneven.

### Corner cutting

Corner cutting is how far off-axis a layer can be before it jams. A cube that cuts well forgives a slightly crooked turn and keeps going. Reverse corner cutting, the ability to turn when a layer is far enough out that the pieces nearly separate, is a bonus. Good corner cutting is a large part of why a speedcube feels so much better than the original.

### Lubrication

Lubricant matters more than most beginners expect. A small amount on the inside surfaces can transform a scratchy cube, and different weights suit different tastes: thin lubricants feel fast and light, thicker ones feel dampened and controlled. Use lubricants made for speedcubes, since household products can damage the plastic. Add a little at a time, work it in by turning the cube, and stop before it feels slippery. Over-lubing is easy and harder to undo than under-lubing.

## Smart cubes

A smart cube is a speedcube with sensors and Bluetooth built in. It reports every turn to an app on your phone or computer, which can:

- time your solves without touching a timer
- record a full replay and move-by-move reconstruction of each solve
- show you move counts, turning speed, and which step took the longest
- let you follow along with tutorials and train specific cases

They are a good fit if you like data, want to study your own solves, or want to practice against others online. They are not necessary for improving, and they are usually heavier than a standard speedcube and cost noticeably more. A smart cube is a nice upgrade after you have a standard cube you like, not a good first purchase.

Smart cubes are not allowed in official competition, so you will want a regular speedcube if you plan to compete. See Article 3 of the [WCA Regulations](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#article-3-puzzles) for what is legal at competitions.

## Buying by budget

Prices change often, so rather than naming models, here is what to expect at each tier.

- **Entry level.** Most of what a beginner needs: a smooth, light, adjustable cube, usually with magnets. Corner cutting is decent and the cube may feel a little less refined. This tier is plenty for learning and for a first year or more of practice.
- **Mid-range.** More polished feel, better corner cutting, more consistent quality, and often more magnet options and adjustment parts. This is the sweet spot for people who practice regularly.
- **Flagship.** The most refined turning, the most adjustability, and sometimes extra features such as swappable magnet strengths or dual adjustment systems. The improvement over a good mid-range cube is real but smaller than the price difference suggests.

Brands such as GAN, MoYu, and QiYi (including its X-Man line) are well-established makers, and each offers cubes across the tiers. Names and lineups change, so check recent reviews and community discussion before you buy.

## 2x2 and big cubes

**2x2.** Every point above applies to a 2x2, except that it has no center caps to tension, so adjustment is usually done with screws near the corners or not at all. A cube that does not pop and cuts corners well matters even more here, since there are fewer pieces to hold it together.

**4x4 and larger.** Bigger cubes put much more weight on build quality: more pieces mean more places to jam or pop. Choose a reputable model, expect to spend more than for a 3x3, and be ready to tune it. If you are curious about big cubes, start with a 4x4 after you are comfortable on a 3x3.

**Other puzzles.** Pyraminx, Megaminx, Skewb, and Square-1 are all official events with their own speedcube versions. The same ideas apply.

## A first cube

Choose by what you need, not by name:

- **If you are just learning,** pick an inexpensive, adjustable, magnetic 3x3 from a known brand. It does not need to be the cheapest, and it does not need to be the best.
- **If you plan to practice a lot,** choose a mid-range cube, and expect to tune its tensions and lubricate it.
- **If you plan to compete,** make sure it is legal under the WCA Regulations, and practice on the same cube you will bring.
- **If you like to analyze your solves,** consider a smart cube as a second purchase, once you have a regular one you enjoy.

Whatever you buy, give a cube a few days. It will feel different once it is broken in, and after you have adjusted it to your fingers.

## Where to buy

Dedicated speedcube shops carry current models, parts, and lubricant, and are the safest place to buy. The [resources page](../../resources/) lists shops, along with the communities where you can ask for opinions before you spend money.
